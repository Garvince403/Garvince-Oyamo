Blog Topics & Homepage Structure

Planning sample based on the “Blogger.com” and “Main Categories” source documents. These are editorial planning materials—not finished homepage copy. The outline below makes their topic-priority and site-taxonomy purpose easier to review.

Purpose of the planning documents

The source materials bring together two connected parts of a blog strategy: a prioritized list of search-focused topics and a proposed set of main categories for organizing the site. Used together, they help a blog plan what to publish and give readers a clear path to related content.

Homepage structure

1. A focused introduction

Open with a concise statement of what the publication covers and who it serves. Follow with a clear route into the main subject areas rather than asking visitors to browse an undifferentiated list of posts.

2. Featured and recent articles

Give priority topics prominent placement, then make the latest articles easy to scan. Each preview should use a specific headline, a short description, and a direct link to the complete article.

3. Category-led browsing

Use the main categories as the primary navigation for evergreen content. Supporting topics can sit beneath those categories so related articles stay grouped without making the homepage crowded.

4. A useful next step

Close article previews and category pages with a relevant next action—such as reading a related guide, exploring a service page, or contacting the business—rather than relying on a generic call to action.

Example editorial topic clusters

The selected portfolio drafts show how one publication can support several complementary content formats:

  • Air conditioners: product reviews, system comparisons, feature explainers, and buyer guidance.
  • Heat pumps: cold-climate performance, system selection, efficiency, and homeowner questions.
  • Local services: city-specific service pages that explain the work, process, and next step for prospective customers.
  • Career advice: practical resources for job seekers, including resume guidance and recruiter interviews.

Turning a topic list into a publishing plan

Start with the priority order in the topic-planning document. Assign each idea to its closest main category, group overlapping ideas into topic clusters, and identify which pieces should be comprehensive guides versus shorter supporting articles. Before publishing, check that each topic answers a distinct reader question and links to useful related content.

How to use the taxonomy

Keep category labels broad enough to hold a useful collection of articles, but specific enough that readers can predict what they will find. Use individual article topics for narrower questions. Review the category list as the library grows so the navigation reflects the actual content rather than an aspirational inventory.

Editorial note

This sample describes the purpose and practical application of the selected planning documents. It is an outline, not a claim that the source documents contain this exact homepage copy or taxonomy.
